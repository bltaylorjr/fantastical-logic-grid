1. The short answer

Keep the whole game free, and add an optional Supporter tier at $2.99 a month or $19.99 a year, a one-time $4.99 Founder unlock for the first year, and $1.99–$3.99 puzzle packs. From about 25,000 monthly players up, fewer than 1 player in 150 needs to pay for the game to cover all of its costs, including your build tools and help. Below that, running costs are $0, and about 1 player in 100 paying covers your tools from about 5,000 players.

Why

  • Running costs stay tiny. The servers cost nothing until about 10,000 monthly players, $5 a month until about 50,000, and about {{INFRA500}} a month at 500,000. That is about $1.30 per 1,000 players.
  • People and tools are the real cost. Your design and build tools, art, help with name approvals and reports, and security are about 70–99% of the total at every size. Pricing should be set to cover those, not just servers.
  • Two costs could explode if they are not kept capped: server voices and sign-ins. Uncapped premium voices would cost thousands a month at 100,000 players. The game already caps them and uses the free on-device voice for everyone else; keep it that way and make premium voices a Supporter perk with a monthly allowance. Firebase sign-ins start charging at about 167,000 monthly players.
  • The market sits between $2.99 and $4.99. Puzzle Baron charges $4.99 a month, Logic Daily sells $1.99 packs and a $4.49 option, Penny Dell sells 30 puzzles for $3.99, and Everyday Grids charges $14.99 a year to remove ads. $2.99 a month is a friendly step below the leader, with no ads anywhere.

Three things to do before the game grows past 10,000 players (all free or nearly free)

  1. Move the big files off GitHub Pages. The intro video (25 MB) and the voice engine (21 MB) will use up the Pages 100 GB a month allowance at about 10,000 players. Serving them from the Cloudflare Worker's static files is free with no limit. GitHub's rules also say Pages is not for running an online business, so once money is involved, the whole site is safer on Cloudflare Pages (free, commercial use allowed, works with your domain without moving it to Cloudflare).
  2. Move player emails off Gmail before the daily email passes about 500 a day. Amazon SES costs about $0.10 per 1,000 emails. costs money
  3. Switch Cloudflare to the $5 Workers Paid plan at about 10,000 monthly players. One plan covers the Worker, the database and higher limits. costs money The admin dashboard's free-limit alerts already warn at 70%.

Everything marked costs money needs your OK first, under the free-only rule. Nothing in this report has been bought or switched on.

3. When each free limit runs out

The chart shows the number of monthly active players at which each free limit is reached. The model assumes a quarter of monthly players play on a given day (normal for a daily-puzzle game), 25 server requests and 15 database writes per daily player, about 10 MB of website download per monthly player, and 30% of players signing in. Busy days are counted at 1.5 times a normal day, so the limits are reached a little before the averages say.

{{LIMITS}}
Green: a free fix exists. Orange: covered by one $5 a month plan or pennies. Red: starts billing per player. Log scale.

What happens at each limit

  • About 10,000 players: website bandwidth and Gmail. Both have a free or nearly free fix (see section 1). These come first because the video and voice engine are large downloads.
  • 11,000–50,000 players: Cloudflare's daily limits. Requests, database writes, database size and reads all move to the same $5 a month Workers Paid plan. Without it, the server starts refusing requests on busy days.
  • About 40,000 players: Workers AI. New pictures and translations start to cost cents a month.
  • About 53,000 players: the $5 plan's included requests. Each extra million requests costs $0.30, which is about $1.90 for every extra 10,000 players.
  • About 167,000 players: Firebase sign-ins. This is the first cost that grows by the player: about $137 a month at 250,000 players and $505 at 500,000. A sign-in system built into the Worker (email links or passkeys) would make this free again; it is worth building at around 100,000 players.
  • Server voices never run out, because they are capped. When the caps are reached, players hear the free on-device voice instead.

5. The costs to keep capped

Server voices

The natural server voices are the one feature that could cost real money fast. The table shows the monthly bill if the caps were removed and 1 in 10 daily players heard a new half puzzle read aloud each day (about 750 characters or 50 seconds of speech).

{{VOICES}}

Keep the caps on. Today the game spends $0 on voices: it uses Gemini's free tier up to 300 new clips a day, Chirp up to 900,000 characters a month, and the free Kokoro voice on the player's device after that. Clips are saved and reused, so the daily puzzle is voiced once for everyone. If you want more premium voice time, give it to Supporters with a monthly allowance, and raise the caps only by what supporter income pays for. Gemini's price doubles on January 1, 2027, so revisit then.

Other costs that grow with success

  • Sign-ins past 167,000 players (section 3). Plan a free Worker-based sign-in around 100,000 players.
  • Making the repository private (being weighed in "Protect the game from copying"): GitHub Pages from a private repository needs GitHub Pro at $4 a month, and Actions get 2,000 free minutes a month. The nightly checks, video builds and reports can use more than that, about $5–$20 a month extra. Moving the website to Cloudflare Pages avoids the $4. costs money
  • Code scanning is free on a public repository. On a private one, GitHub's code scanning costs $30 per person a month; free tools such as Semgrep Community can replace it.
  • Mobile apps (paused): Apple charges $99 a year and takes 15% of sales for small businesses; Google Play is $25 once and 15%. The Microsoft Store is free for individual developers.
  • Moderation time grows with players because typed screen names wait for approval. Suggested names auto-approve, so steering players toward them keeps this cost down.

What that means

  • Players of logic puzzles are used to paying $2–$5 for a pack and about $5 a month at the top end. A yearly price of $15–$20 is normal.
  • This game's edge is that every puzzle is new and nothing is locked. Locking puzzles behind a paywall would throw that away. Selling extras and thanks keeps it.
  • For free games with an optional paid tier, 1–3% of monthly players usually pay; tip jars alone usually get under 0.5%. This report uses 0.5%, 1.5% and 3%.

Rules that protect players and the budget

  • Never lock a core feature or puzzle. Paid things are extras, thanks, and convenience.
  • No ads before 50,000 monthly players, and only if supporter income falls short. Ads earn about $1–$5 per 1,000 visits and hurt a calm puzzle game.
  • Use a seller that handles sales tax for you (Lemon Squeezy or Paddle) for anything sold. Tips can go through GitHub Sponsors.
  • Check prices once a year and when a vendor changes prices (next: Gemini voices on January 1, 2027).
  • Keep one switch for each paid extra in the admin Settings, as with the Support link, so anything can be turned off without a release.

How this fits the work in progress. The "Prepare freemium launch package" work is building three money options for you to choose from: a tip jar with a Supporter badge, a Ko-fi membership, and a Lemon Squeezy one-time unlock. This report recommends starting with the tip jar and Supporter badge (no cost, no code beyond the badge), then adding the $2.99 / $19.99 Supporter tier and the $4.99 Founder unlock through Lemon Squeezy once there are about 2,000 monthly players to sell to. The Support the game switch from the freemium launch is already in the admin dashboard.
