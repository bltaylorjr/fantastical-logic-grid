            How to play The Fantastical Logic Grid

            Captions are on. Use the CC button in the player to turn them off.

            Read the transcript

            Welcome to The Fantastical Logic Grid. Every case is a brand new story, and you are the detective. Each puzzle is a logic grid wrapped in a story. The story sets up a mystery, the clues give you the facts, and the grid is where you work out the answer. Every story is written fresh for you and never repeated. And every puzzle has exactly one answer you can reach without guessing.

            Every day there is also a new Daily puzzle, the same one for everyone. Solve it each day to build a streak. Here's how to play. Start on the New puzzle screen and pick a difficulty. Basic and Easy are perfect for beginners. MENSA is for true masterminds.

            Next, choose a theme, like Mystery, Fantasy, Sci-Fi, Around Town, National Parks or Vacation Destinations. Or pick Surprise me. Most themes also have topics, like which holiday or which kind of mystery. Pick one, or leave it on Any. Then pick a size. More categories and more items make a bigger, harder grid.

            Full experience adds scenes, characters and sound. Silent mode keeps only the story, the grid, the clues and the answer key. Press Generate puzzle, and your story is written in a moment. Here's our case: The Spooky Keys. The buttons along the top help you as you play. Read the story first. It tells you who is involved, and it ends with the question you need to answer. Here, it's: Who arrived at 9:45 PM? You can also press Read the story aloud to hear it.

            Select a character's portrait to hear the clues they remember. Every clue is also in the clue list. To hear every clue in order, press Read the clues aloud. Each character tells their own clue, and the clue being read lights up. On your first Easy puzzle, coach tips appear above the grid to guide your first moves. Now the grid. Each square is where two things meet, like a ghost hunter and an arrival time. Your job is to decide whether they go together.

            Click or tap a square once to mark an X. X means ruled out: these two don't go together. Tap again for an O. O means confirmed: these two are a match. Tap a third time to clear the square. Let's solve it together, starting with the simplest clues. Clue 1 says the ghost hunter who carried the Flashlight arrived at 10:00 PM. Mark an O there.

            Clues 2 and 3 each rule out pairs directly. Mark an X for each one. Clue 4 says the ghost hunter who carried the Flashlight is Felix or Hugo, so no other ghost hunter is possible. Clue 5 says the ghost hunter who carried the Thermometer did not arrive at 9:30 PM. Mark an X there. Clue 6 says Felix arrived at 9:45 PM. Mark an O there.

            Clue 7 says the ghost hunter who carried the Compass did not arrive at 9:45 PM. Mark an X there. Felix arrived at 9:45 PM, so Felix cannot go with any other arrival, and no other ghost hunter can go with 9:45 PM. Each row and each column in a section has exactly one match, so everything else in that line is an X. The ghost hunter who carried the Flashlight arrived at 10:00 PM, so Flashlight cannot go with any other arrival, and no other gear can go with 10:00 PM. 9:15 PM is the only arrival left for Thermometer. Mark an O there. When only one square is left in a row or column, it must be the answer.

            In the full experience, each finished section also reveals more of the scene. Stuck? Press Hint. The first hint shows you which clue to use, and names the trick that cracks it. Press Which cell? for the exact square, then Explain why for the full reasoning. It ends with a short lesson on how that trick works, so you can spot it yourself next time. You can even let it mark the square for you.

            Press Check at any time to see whether any of your marks are wrong. Made a mistake? Undo and Redo take back any move, as many times as you like. Want a fresh start? Press Start over. It clears your marks and resets the timer, hints and checks. Your notes stay unless you choose to clear them, and Undo can bring everything back. Prefer words to grids? List view lets you choose each match from a menu. The game also works fully by keyboard and with a screen reader.

            When you think you know, answer the questions under the clues, and press Check answers. Want to jot something down? Press My notes and type. Press Save to keep them, or Close to get back to the puzzle. Only you can see your notes, even in a race. Keep going the same way, clue by clue, until the whole grid is filled in. Case closed! Felix arrived at 9:45 PM, so Felix was behind the ring of keys all along! It was a prank with a bedsheet and a fan. You also see your time and a suggestion for your next puzzle.

            Solving puzzles earns badges, like First case closed. Press Share my result to send friends a picture of your win. It never gives the answers away. If you like, rate the puzzle from 1 to 5 stars (5 is excellent), and say whether it felt too easy, just right or too hard. The game learns from your answers and tunes your next puzzle to suit you. You can print any puzzle, blank or with your marks, with the answer key on its own page. Your progress saves automatically. Come back any time and pick up where you left off, from Saved puzzles.

            My stats and badges, in the Menu, shows your totals, best times, favorite themes, your daily streak and every badge to earn. Press Help to see every keyboard shortcut. And How to play, at the top of every page, plays this video again. Play in light or dark colors, or in high contrast. And Silent mode is always there when you just want the puzzle.

            Scoring and race rules

            Every puzzle you solve earns points, alone or in a race. In a race everyone gets the same puzzle, and the lowest race time wins. You need an account and a screen name to earn points or race. Playing alone never needs one.

            Once you sign in, the game asks you to choose a screen name (or uses the one your account already has), and it shows at the top of the screen. If you are not signed in, you play as Guest.

            How points are awarded

            Points = difficulty base × size × speed × help, rounded, and at least 1.

            • Difficulty base: Basic 10, Easy 20, Medium 35, Hard 55, Expert 80, MENSA 110.
            • Size: bigger grids are worth more. A 3 × 4 grid counts as 1, a 4 × 4 grid as 1.4, a 6 × 7 grid as 3.9.
            • Speed: matching the usual time for the puzzle (shown on the New puzzle screen) counts as 1. Solving in under about half the usual time earns up to 1.5. Very slow solves still get at least 0.6.
            • Help: each hint takes away 15% and each check 5%, but you always keep at least a quarter of the points. Each step of a hint (the nudge, the clue, the cell) counts as one hint.
            • A revealed solution earns nothing, and neither does a puzzle you started over. In a race, Start over clears your marks but the race clock, hints and checks keep counting. Each puzzle counts once, and up to 40 solo puzzles a day count.

            Race rules

            1. Start: a race uses your New puzzle choices. Share the 6-character code or link. You choose whether strangers can join through Find a race.
            2. Join: with the code, the link, or Find a race. A race is open to new players for 7 days and takes up to 20 players.
            3. Challenges: anyone in a race can challenge a player by screen name. The challenge waits in the game under Play against others until they accept (and join the race) or say no thanks. Each player can be challenged once per race, and you can send up to 30 challenges a day. Players can turn challenges off in their screen name window.
            4. Race time = solve time + 1 minute per hint + 15 seconds per check. The clock runs while the puzzle is on screen.
            5. Places go from the lowest race time. Equal times share a place.
            6. Finishing: your answers are checked, and a result is sent once and cannot be changed. Results count for 14 days after the race starts.
            7. Giving up: revealing the solution counts as giving up and earns no points. You have 10 seconds to undo it.
            8. Points: a solved race earns points the same way as a solo puzzle.
            9. Race wins: you have a win while you are in first place and at least one other player has solved the race. Someone who finishes later with a lower time can take the win.

            The scoreboard

            • This week starts on Monday at 00:00 UTC. All time never resets.
            • Players are ranked by points. A tie goes to more race wins, then to whoever scored first.
            • The top 50 are shown, and your own place is always shown. The board can be up to 30 seconds behind.
            • Leave the scoreboard (in the screen name window) removes your name, points and race results. To stay signed in you then choose a new name, or you can sign out and play as Guest.

            Read a clue, then mark the grid. X means two things do not go together. O means they do. Each row in a sub-grid has exactly one O. When the grid is full, answer the story questions.
